Why investing

I've liked working with numbers since middle school algebra, there was something satisfying about problems that actually had a clear, provable answer. Finance grabbed me for the opposite reason, being that so much of financial analysis is built on assumptions or expectations about the future. I found it super interesting that the answer is still not definite.

I feel that could be the fun and exciting part of this industry. You're not just solving for one right answer anymore, instead you're expected to make a call, to build a case for what you think will happen, and being upfront about how much you could be wrong. That combination of real analytical work but still having to make a strategic bet anyway is what has kept me interested lately.

What I've been learning

Doing the Bloomberg Market Concepts certification was the first time I actually got to use a Bloomberg Terminal, and it changed how I think about decisions that look simple from the outside. Something like a stock price or an economic indicator seems like just a number, but there's this huge amount of real-time data like rates, spreads, macro releases, and company-specific news all going into it at once. It made me realize how much is actually happening behind a decision that gets summarized in one headline.

That's part of why I started Wall Street Prep's modeling course — I want to get past reading about how valuation works and actually build the mechanics myself. I'm early in it, but even in the first few hours it's clear how much of the work is about beginning a problem correctly before you ever get to an answer.

Something I've been curious about

I've been thinking lately about how AI could affect trading and banking roles. If algorithms were made without the emotional bias that a human has, but now with the ability to read and analyze breaking news or surprises within seconds, that is now a major difference when competing against a person. I wonder if that would make a market more rational and completely different, or would the edge just simply move to whoever has the fastest system. Now the emotional bias could still be there, but now it would be up to whoever built the system or algorithm and decided what its priorities are.
